Motion studies · Jacob Boehme

Boehme,
Jacob

Görlitz · 1575–1624

Three ways of animating one entry. The shoemaker’s vision of 1600, the heliocentric Aurora of 1612, and the dark–light triptych of The Three Principles of 1618. Each study uses a different visual language: woodcut line-drawing, a copperplate cosmological diagram, and an alchemical emblem.

“the gleam of light … in a tin or pewter vessel gave him the impression of looking into a concealed ‘center of nature’” — the entry, on the experience of 1600

I

The Pewter Gleam

Görlitz, 1600 · SVG woodcut, line-drawing

SVG · stroke animation · paper grain

“In the famous version recounted by Franckenberg, the young shoemaker was seated in his shop when the gleam of light (presumably a reflection of the sun) in a tin or pewter vessel gave him the impression of looking into a concealed ‘center of nature’…”

The drawing follows the entry’s own account: the leaded window, the shoemaker’s bench and the pewter vessel. The radiant diagram is not a portrait of the vision but a diagram of what the text calls the “center of nature”: concentric spheres, the cross of the four directions, and seven marked qualities.

“Aurora also accepts the symbolic and real centrality of the sun in the solar system. … Therefore, the seven qualities or forces are elaborated into a microcosm of divine being, a kind of dynamic and nature-generating atom of all things and processes…”

The seven rings are the seven Quellgeister or source-spirits of Aurora, not literal planets. The central triangle is the entry’s “visible symbol of the Trinity”; the orbiting numbers correspond to the sequence given in the text. Click or tap the stage to send a flash through the diagram.

III

Three Principles

1618 · SVG alchemical triptych

SVG · emblem · interactive panels

Click a panel to read the passage it translates.

“The ‘principles’ of his title find their way into his work … as an interpretation of the Paracelsian tria prima of Sulphur, Mercury, and Salt. In connecting these three alchemical substances … with three aspects of the omnipresent trinitarian deity…”

The triptych is built from the entry’s own images: the dark fire world, the light world rising out of it, the tree of the earthly world, the androgynous first Adam, and the tria prima. Click a panel to see the sentence it illustrates.
